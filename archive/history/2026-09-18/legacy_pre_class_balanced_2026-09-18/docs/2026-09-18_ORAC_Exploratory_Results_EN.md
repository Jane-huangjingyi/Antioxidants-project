# Exploratory Historical ORAC Comparison Results

The formal Tier A standard was retained, while the former Tier B was split into B1 (eligible for exploratory numerical comparison) and B2 (supportive quantitative evidence only). Among 17 pilot compounds, 11 have at least one B1 record and four have at least two independent B1 records. The dataset contains 16 records: Tier A=0, B1=15, B2=1, C=0, and X=0. All 11 former Tier B records were reassigned to B1. Targeted searching added B1 records for catechin, epicatechin, trans-resveratrol, and ferulic acid; a quantitative sulforaphane report was assigned B2. No record for EGCG, kaempferol, lycopene, beta-carotene, or lutein simultaneously met the requirements for a traceable primary ORAC-FL result, reliable mol TE/mol conversion, and adequate interference assessment.

The H-ORAC arm now supports a meaningful but limited exploratory comparison across nine compounds. Descriptive medians are quercetin 11.95, epicatechin 10.0, catechin 7.88, caffeic acid 6.315, trans-resveratrol 5.811, chlorogenic acid 3.14, ferulic acid 2.771, gallic acid 1.25, and L-ascorbic acid 0.74 mol TE/mol. This order describes included historical literature only and must not be presented as a ranking of the strongest antioxidants. Catechin, quercetin, caffeic acid, and L-ascorbic acid have two independent records; all other estimates are single-study exploratory estimates.

Within-compound heterogeneity is material. Quercetin spans 7.28–16.62 mol TE/mol and has the largest absolute and relative spread; caffeic acid spans 4.37–8.26, L-ascorbic acid 0.53–0.95, and catechin 6.76–9.0. Catechin has the smallest range relative to its median, but n=2 for every multi-study compound, so precision or stability claims would be premature. Plausible sources include pH, fluorescein and AAPH concentrations, integration cut-off, solvent, chemical form, and linearity range.

The L-ORAC arm remains too sparse for a robust within-arm ranking. Alpha- and gamma-tocopherol each have one B1 value from the same methodological paper, so the figure is labelled historical evidence coverage and reported values. No carotenoid has B1 evidence. Poor aqueous solubility, aggregation, photolability, inner-filter effects, and isomerism demand compound-specific QC. These tocopherol values must not be merged with H-ORAC or generalized to carotenoids.

Sulforaphane is B2 only because the pure-compound comparison cannot be reliably normalized to mol TE/mol and because its main redox relevance is electrophilic stress/Nrf2 regulation, which ORAC may underrepresent. Existing ORAC evidence also cannot represent the full redox biology of carotenoids. Resveratrol is B1 but remains sensitive to solvent and cyclodextrin conditions.

The evidence is sufficient for a supervisor update and a provisional candidate shortlist for further retrieval and repeat-testing plans, but not for a formal Top20. Priority standardized retesting should include quercetin, caffeic acid, and L-ascorbic acid because of historical dispersion; EGCG, kaempferol, and resveratrol because of high relevance but incomplete or solubility-sensitive evidence; alpha-/gamma-tocopherol; and lycopene, beta-carotene, and lutein under an L-ORAC method with optical blanks, aggregation controls, and isomer control. CUPRAC coverage supports its future role as secondary evidence, but the current audit is presence-only and cannot support quantitative ranking.
