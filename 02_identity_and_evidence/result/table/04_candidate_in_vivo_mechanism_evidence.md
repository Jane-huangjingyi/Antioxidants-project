# In vivo antioxidant mechanism evidence for the 12 retained candidates

This targeted evidence map covers all 12 candidates retained under the first-round ORAC-FL rank-stability rule. It contains 28 observations from 26 primary publications, including 19 animal in vivo observations, eight human in vivo observations and one ex vivo observation. The human records cover six compounds. Twenty-four publications have a DOI. Two have links to the original report and no DOI was located. Five observations use the abstract alone. Source scope, unresolved fields and the interpretation supported by each comparison are recorded in the [CSV](04_candidate_in_vivo_mechanism_evidence.csv).

The candidate IDs come from the [first-round ranking](../../../03_candidate_selection/result/table/04_figure1_candidate_ranks.csv). The evidence scan used compound names, reported forms and common aliases with terms for oxidative stress, in vivo models and redox pathway nodes, followed by DOI or PubMed linking to original reports. Representative reports were selected for source traceability, model relevance, clear compound form and informative positive, null or adverse findings. The targeted scan permits older mechanistic studies and has no complete retrieval denominator. It should be cited as a provisional evidence map and not used to estimate prevalence or to score mechanisms. A paper can contribute separate comparisons from the same study, as shown by shared DOI values.

## Reading the map

The human follow-up prioritized compound-specific interventions with an explicit comparator or dose group. It retained null endpoints and one purified chlorogenic acid trial as adjacent vascular evidence. In rows without an assigned protein, the reported endpoint and interpretation boundary govern how to read the broad `PATHWAY_MODULATION` label. Oxidative biomarkers and vascular function inform distinct questions, and plasma ORAC reflects the sampled matrix. For the new abstract-level entries, verify the study arms, assay methods and numerical contrasts before reusing effect sizes.

- **Functional support in an animal model.** Quercetin's NRF2 knockout comparison concerns liver injury endpoints. Its hepatic redox readouts are documented in a separate wild-type comparison. For oral glutathione, CFTR genotype relates to glutathione availability. These are different endpoint types.
- **Model-specific support.** Chlorogenic acid's dietary mouse comparison documents vascular redox and protein readouts. Its NRF2 perturbation was performed in isolated aortic rings and is labeled ex vivo. Oral glutathione's healthy-adult trial found no significant improvement in the measured oxidative or glutathione biomarkers in the tested setting. Acute high-dose genistein also yielded an unfavorable result in the exercise study.
- **Evidence status.** Readout changes document expression, localization, activity or redox markers. Loss-of-function supports functional participation in the tested model. No included row establishes direct binding. Gulo denotes ascorbate biosynthesis status in the vitamin C model. It is not a protein target of the intervention.

`reported_material` describes the experimental intervention. Catechin hydrate is visible as a specific form, hesperetin and hesperidin keep separate rows, and no active metabolite is assigned without source evidence. `UNRESOLVED` flags a detail that remains to be checked. 23 entries remain `NOT_REVIEWED`; ME12-01/02/11/12 were researcher-CONFIRMED and ME12-05 was researcher-reviewed as KEEP_OUT (conservative exclusion) on 2026-11-26 (`change_applied=NO`) — see the [adjudication record](audit/Researcher_Adjudication_Record_2026-11-26.csv). The mechanism pilot and ORAC-FL ranking remain governed by their respective evidence sets.
