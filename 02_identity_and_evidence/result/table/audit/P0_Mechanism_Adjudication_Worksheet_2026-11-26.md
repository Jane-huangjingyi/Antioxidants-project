## 1. Quercetin — ME12-01 / ME12-02

Source: Jin Y et al. "Quercetin attenuates toosendanin-induced hepatotoxicity through inducing the Nrf2/GCL/GSH antioxidant signaling pathway." Acta Pharmacol Sin 2019;40:75–85. DOI 10.1038/s41401-018-0024-8, PMC6318309.

### Verified design (Methods, "Experimental animals" / "Animal treatments")
- WT C57BL/6 male mice (16–20 g, Shanghai Laboratory Animal Center). Nrf2−/− C57BL/6 generated by TALEN (SiDanSai Biotech); allele sequence-validated (2-bp GA deletion at ORF 346–347 → frameshift/early stop); Nrf2 protein deletion Western-confirmed (Supplementary Figure 1).
- Protection experiment (Fig. 7): six WT groups (n=8 each) — normal control, vehicle (10% propylene glycol), TSN, TSN+quercetin 40 mg/kg, TSN+quercetin 80 mg/kg, quercetin 80 mg/kg alone. Quercetin i.g. × 7 consecutive days; TSN 10 mg/kg i.p. 1 h after the seventh quercetin dose; sacrifice 24 h after TSN.
- Knockout experiment (Fig. 8): Nrf2+/+ and Nrf2−/−, three groups per genotype (vehicle, TSN, TSN+quercetin), n=6/group; quercetin 80 mg/kg i.g. × 7 days; same TSN challenge/timing.
- Quercetin from Sigma; TSN purity >98% (Shanghai Tauto). All figure data are mean ± SEM.

### ME12-01 (WT redox readouts) — verdict proposal: CONFIRM_ATTRIBUTION_AS_RECORDED
- Fig. 7 caption (verbatim): "c Liver GSH content (n = 6). d Liver ROS level (n = 6). e Liver MDA content (n = 6). f Hepatic mRNA expression of Nrf2 (n = 3). g Hepatic protein expression of Nrf2… h Quantitative densitometric analysis of Nrf2 (n = 4)."
- Results (verbatim): "Quercetin (40, 80 mg/kg) also reversed the decreased liver GSH content and increased hepatic ROS formation induced by TSN in mice (Fig. 7c, d). Quercetin (80 mg/kg) reduced the TSN-induced increase in the liver MDA content in mice (Fig. 7e)."
- **Match to CSV:** the row's endpoints (hepatic GSH; ROS; MDA) map exactly to Fig. 7c–e in WT animals; dose 40/80 mg/kg, oral gavage, 7 days, 24 h sampling, TSN-alone comparator all confirmed. READOUT_ONLY is correct (no loss-of-function in this figure).
- **Refinements to record:** (a) the redox markers are Fig. 7c–e specifically; Fig. 7f–h are Nrf2 expression readouts (also WT) — the current locator "Fig. 7c–h" is broader than the endpoint list; (b) the MDA reversal is stated at 80 mg/kg only; (c) error type is SEM (n=6 for redox), not SD.
- Boundary stays: oxidative endpoints measured in WT; genetic dependence is the separate Fig. 8 contrast.

### ME12-02 (WT vs Nrf2-KO injury endpoints) — verdict proposal: CONFIRM_ATTRIBUTION_AS_RECORDED
- Fig. 8 caption (verbatim): "C57BL/6 wild-type (WT) and Nrf2 knock-out (KO) mice… a Serum ALT activity. b Serum AST activity. c Histological evaluation of liver injury… mean ± SEM (n = 6)… & P < 0.05 vs. WT mice."
- Results (verbatim): "quercetin (80 mg/kg) significantly reduced the increased serum ALT and AST activities induced by TSN in wild-type mice, but quercetin (80 mg/kg) had no inhibitory effect on the TSN-induced increase in serum ALT and AST activities in Nrf2 knock-out mice. Moreover, the TSN-induced increase in serum ALT and AST activities was significantly higher in Nrf2 knock-out mice than in wild-type mice (Fig. 8a, b)." Histology: "quercetin prevented TSN-induced liver damage in wild-type mice… However, such protection was diminished in Nrf2 knock-out mice (Fig. 8c)."
- **Match to CSV:** serum ALT/AST + histology under WT-vs-KO, 80 mg/kg, within-genotype challenged controls — all confirmed. FUNCTIONAL_PARTICIPATION_SUPPORTED is justified for liver-injury endpoints (loss-of-function abolishes ALT/AST protection, diminishes histological protection; injury worse in KO).
- **Boundary confirmed exactly:** Fig. 8 does NOT measure GSH/MDA/ROS, so it does not test Nrf2 dependence of the redox markers; no direct-binding experiment exists in this paper. The CSV boundary sentence is accurate and should be kept.
- Refinement: specify TALEN-generated constitutive Nrf2−/− (not siRNA); n=6/group; SEM.

**Disposition options for the researcher (both rows):**
1. CONFIRM_ATTRIBUTION_AS_RECORDED (with the locator/error-type refinements above) — rows stay as mechanism evidence, NOT_REVIEWED→reviewed per your governance; manuscript may cite WT redox reversal (Fig. 7c–e) and Nrf2-dependent injury protection (Fig. 8a–c) with the stated boundaries.
2. RELABEL_LOCATOR_ONLY — keep dispositions, tighten ME12-01 locator to Fig. 7c–e (redox) / 7f–h (Nrf2 expression) and add SEM/n.
3. HOLD — if you require the clean JATS XML or the supplementary KO-validation blot before any use.

## 2. Chlorogenic acid — ME12-11 / ME12-12

Source: Wang D et al. "Dietary chlorogenic acid ameliorates oxidative stress and improves endothelial function in diabetic mice via Nrf2 activation." J Int Med Res 2021;49:300060520985363. DOI 10.1177/0300060520985363, PMC7829538 (OA).

### Verified design (Methods)
- Animals: male Nrf2−/− (Jackson), db/db (leprdb) and age-matched C57BL/KsJ lean controls (Nanjing). Dietary arm: normal chow ± 0.02% CGA (Sigma-Aldrich) × 12 weeks.
- Cells: HUVECs, NG 5 mM vs HG 30 mM (± mannitol osmotic control), CGA 10 µM in DMSO; Nrf2 siRNA vs scrambled (Santa Cruz), 20 µg, 6 h/day × 2 days.
- Aortic rings: WT and Nrf2−/− rings dissected, incubated 24 h in DMEM + 10% FBS + antibiotics, then wire myograph; ACh 10⁻⁹–10⁻⁵ M (endothelium-dependent), NTG (endothelium-independent); Phe pre-contraction.
- Fig. 5 caption fixes the ex vivo treatment window: "HG exposure (30 mM, 12 hours)… CGA (10 µM, 12 hours)". All figures mean ± SEM (n=6); Fig. 7 "six rings (n = 6 mice per group)".

### ME12-11 (dietary db/db, in vivo) — verdict proposal: CONFIRM_ATTRIBUTION_AS_RECORDED
- Fig. 6 caption: "(d–e) Superoxide anion production assessed via dihydroethidium (DHE) staining in aortas from lean littermate control C57BL/KsJ mice and db/db mice fed either a normal diet (db/db Cont) or a normal diet containing 0.02% CGA (db/db CGA). (f–g) NO production assessed via diaminofluorescein-2 diacetate (DAF-2DA) staining…"
- Fig. 8 caption: vascular Nrf2, P22phox/P47phox/P67phox and nitrotyrosine western blots in db/db Cont vs db/db CGA vs lean controls.
- Results (verbatim): "Dietary CGA attenuated ROS production and increased NO levels in the endothelium of db/db mice (Figure 6d–6g)… Western blotting revealed that CGA feeding attenuated downregulation of Nrf2, upregulation of P22phox, P47phox, and P67phox, and generation of nitrotyrosine in db/db mice (Figure 8a–8f)."
- **Match to CSV:** ANIMAL_IN_VIVO, dietary 0.02% × 12 weeks, aortic endothelium, DHE/nitrotyrosine/NO endpoints, Figs. 6d–g and 8 — all confirmed. READOUT_ONLY is correct: the dietary arm has no Nrf2-KO group; Nrf2 involvement there is expression association only.
- **Boundary confirmed exactly:** "Nrf2 dependence tested in isolated rings and cells, not in the dietary db/db comparison." Keep.
- Refinements: error type SEM (n=6); Fig. 7 (myograph relaxation after dietary treatment) is an ex vivo measurement of an in vivo intervention — if cited, label it that way; Fig. 6a–c are metabolic endpoints (weight, glucose, HbA1c), not redox.

### ME12-12 (isolated rings, ex vivo) — verdict proposal: CONFIRM_ATTRIBUTION_AS_RECORDED (resolve duration gap)
- Fig. 4 caption: "ROS production… DHE staining in the en face endothelium of aortas from Nrf2 knockout (Nrf2−/−) and matched wild type (WT) mice after treatment with normal glucose (NG), HG or HG+CGA (10 µM)… NO production… DAF-2DA staining." Caption explicitly says "ex vivo".
- Fig. 5 caption: "CGA (10 µM, 12 hours) ameliorated endothelial dysfunction in wild type mouse aortas but not in Nrf2−/− mouse aortas cultured under HG conditions… (c–d) Endothelium-independent relaxation…".
- Results (verbatim): "CGA (10 µM) reversed HG-induced increases in ROS production and decreases in NO levels in aortas from wild-type but not Nrf2−/− mice (Figure 4)… CGA ameliorated impairment of ACh-induced endothelium-dependent relaxation under HG conditions in cultured aortic rings from wild-type (Figure 5a) but not Nrf2−/− mice (Figure 5b). We observed no effect of CGA on NTG-induced endothelium-independent relaxation in either wild-type or Nrf2−/− mice (Figure 5c and 5d)."
- **Match to CSV:** EX_VIVO_SUPPORT, WT vs Nrf2-KO rings, HG incubation, 10 µM, DHE/NO/ACh relaxation, Figs. 4–5, FUNCTIONAL_PARTICIPATION_SUPPORTED — all confirmed.
- **Gap resolved:** the CSV's "exact incubation duration not extracted" → Fig. 5 caption states HG 30 mM and CGA 10 µM for 12 hours; Methods add a 24 h pre-incubation in DMEM+10% FBS before myograph. Fig. 4's DHE/NO treatment duration is not separately stated in the caption (likely the same 12 h HG window; mark as inferred-if-used).
- Refinements: SEM (n=6); NTG independence confirmed; cellular siRNA arm (Figs. 1–3) is separate in vitro evidence, not part of these two rows.

**Disposition options (both rows):**
1. CONFIRM_ATTRIBUTION_AS_RECORDED; fill ME12-12 duration = 12 h HG ± CGA (Fig. 5 caption) + 24 h pre-incubation; note SEM/n=6.
2. RELABEL_EX_VIVO measurement nuance for Fig. 7 if the researcher wants it cited (in vivo intervention, ex vivo readout).
3. HOLD pending figure-image inspection (graphics filenames are in the working-dir extract if you want panel-level confirmation).

## 3. Hesperetin — ME12-05

Source: "Hesperetin Confers Neuroprotection by Regulating Nrf2/TLR4/NF-κB Signaling in an Aβ Mouse Model." Mol Neurobiol 2019;56:6293–6309. DOI 10.1007/s12035-019-1512-7, PMID 30756299. Springer; OA=N, inEPMC=N, hasPDF=N → full text and figures NOT retrievable in this pass.

### What the abstract verifies (and only this)
- Aβ1-42 intracerebroventricular injection; hesperetin injected 24 h later (dose/route/duration NOT in abstract → UNRESOLVED).
- "hesperetin significantly attenuated oxidative stress, as assessed by the expression of Nrf2/HO-1 and LPO and ROS assays, in the hippocampus, cortex, and in vitro HT22 cells." TLR4/p-NF-κB work includes inhibitor experiments in BV-2 cells (separate cellular system).
- The CSV row's ABSTRACT_ONLY status and UNRESOLVED dose/comparator fields are accurate.

### Image-similarity concern — verified verbatim
The retracted melatonin article (PMID 34428482, DOI 10.1016/j.neuro.2021.08.010, Neurotoxicology 87:1–10, 2021) carries this withdrawal text in its record:
> "This article has been retracted at the request of the Editors-in-Chief. **Figure 1C appears similar to** Figure 5h of the article published by Oxidative Medicine and Cellular Longevity 2021 (2021) Article ID 6635552 https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/6635552, Figure 5a of the article published by Cells 10 (2021) 2719 https://doi.org/10.3390/cells10102719 **and Figure 8a of the article published by Molecular Neurobiology 56 (2019) 6293–6309 https://doi.org/10.1007/s12035-019-1512-7.** Although this article was published earlier than the Cells article, the Editors decided to retract this article given concerns about the reliability of the data. Also, sections of panels within Figures 1H and 2G appear similar to each other. The journal records indicated that the names of the authors Reem Alshaman and Muhammad Imran Khan were added to the revised version of the article without exceptional approval by the handling Editor…"

- The hesperetin paper's **Fig. 8a is explicitly named** in the concern (via DOI 10.1007/s12035-019-1512-7). The map's boundary sentence is accurate.
- The retraction notice (PMID 36907799, DOI 10.1016/j.neuro.2023.03.001) retracts the **melatonin article only**. No retraction of the hesperetin paper was found in this pass; being named in another article's image-similarity concern is not itself a retraction, but it is an unresolved integrity flag on the specific panel.
- The similarity claim concerns the melatonin article's Fig. 1C resembling the hesperetin Fig. 8a (and two other papers' panels). Which article is the source of the shared image is NOT established by the notice.

### Verdict proposal: KEEP_OUT_PENDING_VERIFICATION
- Any manuscript claim relying on hesperetin Fig. 8a stays out until the panel is inspected. The abstract-level Nrf2/HO-1/LPO/ROS statement is not itself implicated by the notice (the concern is panel-specific), but the row's quantitative/figure-dependent content cannot be cleared without the PDF.
- **Materials the researcher must supply (access gap):**
  1. Springer PDF of Mol Neurobiol 56:6293–6309 (institutional access) — Fig. 8a image, its legend, and the Results paragraphs citing Fig. 8a.
  2. The exact in vivo dose, route, duration and comparator groups (Methods) to resolve the CSV's UNRESOLVED fields.
  3. Optionally, the other named panels for comparison: Oxid Med Cell Longev 2021;6635552 Fig. 5h (OA, Hindawi — retrievable on request) and Cells 10:2719 Fig. 5a (OA, MDPI — retrievable on request), plus the retracted melatonin Fig. 1C if accessible.
  4. A decision on whether to keep the row as abstract-only context (with the integrity flag) or drop it from the map entirely.
- Do not infer that the hesperetin paper is retracted, and do not infer that Fig. 8a is fraudulent; the verified fact is the editors' similarity concern naming the panel.
