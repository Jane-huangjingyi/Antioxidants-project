# Results 1. Literature-informed prioritization of food-derived antioxidant candidates

## 1.1 Discovery and evidence assembly

The discovery register contained 204 entries and yielded 193 chemical profiles (Figure 1A). The targeted original-evidence release contained 304 observations from 18 DOI-linked reports. Of these reports, 10 were in the primary-window stratum, 1 in the 2016-2019 evidence-gap extension, and 7 provided historical comparison. The report count describes the extraction overlay. Automated retrieval covered a broader corpus whose title, abstract and full-text screening remained incomplete. Study independence and experimental replication were retained as distinct fields (Table S4).

## 1.2 Assay-specific antioxidant activity

There were 225 ranked observations in 20 within-source blocks, including conditional ORAC-FL comparisons for 51 compounds (Figure 1B and Table S2). A comparison block fixes the report, assay, reported protocol, unit and response direction. A block can include unresolved material or dose-scale conditions, which are carried with each observation. The same-source FL and PGR comparison showed substantial reordering, so no common scale was assigned across probes. Crocin lacked a qualified comparison panel.

## 1.3 Model validation and robustness

The expanded conditional FL model used 113 observations across 11 DOI reports. On 75 estimable held-out outcomes, log-scale RMSE was 0.631, compared with 0.676 for the matched mean baseline (Figure 1C and Table S3). The single-paper structure Ridge had scaffold-held-out RMSE 0.945 versus 0.953 for the null. This experiment provides no independent-study evidence of structural transfer. Equal-row and DOI-balanced conditional top-20 sets shared 17 members. The three most consistently high candidates in the fixed component had source-deletion position bounds of Catechin 2-10, Quercetin 2-12, Hesperetin 3-15 (Table S5). These bounds quantify sensitivity to sources, with sampling uncertainty unestimated.

## 1.4 Candidate priorities for aqueous verification

The study proposed 24 compounds for experimental consideration, comprising 6 first-priority, 16 exploratory and 2 deferred candidates (Figure 1D and Table 1). The initial qualification panel comprises Catechin, Epicatechin, Gallic acid, Chlorogenic acid, L-Ascorbic acid, Glutathione. This selection spans flavanols, distinct phenolic acid chemistry, ascorbate and a reduced thiol. Eligibility for aqueous testing requires researcher review and qualification of the exact materials and assay conditions. The prioritization combines quantitative evidence, source reliability and expected assay suitability. It does not assign a pooled potency position.
