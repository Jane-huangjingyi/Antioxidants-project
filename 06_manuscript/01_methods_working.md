# Methods

## Compound identification and literature retrieval

We reconstructed an initial register from an existing class-based inventory of food-associated chemicals, retained previously excluded entries for traceability and added literature-led candidates with documented rationale. The register was reconciled to a profile-level review set using chemical names, formulas, structures, PubChem identifiers and available food-occurrence evidence. We treated distinct stereoisomers, conjugates and salt or hydrate forms separately where the source permitted. Food occurrence and exact-form evidence were recorded independently of assay activity. Unresolved profiles remained visible in the screening set.

The primary search window was 1 January 2020 to 17 November 2026. Searches combined assay-based and compound-class routes with supplementary candidate-name and verified-alias queries. The search included the PubMed ESearch and EFetch export, the Europe PMC cursor export and the available authenticated Web of Science Core Collection export. Query strings, dates, returned counts and batch hashes are provided in the [search audit](../02_identity_and_evidence/05_multidatabase_retrieval/Database_Coverage_Audit.md). Earlier studies were retained for evidence-gap or historical comparisons. First-publication, issue and indexing dates were stored separately. We followed the distinction between retrieval, screening and eligibility counts in PRISMA 2020 [3].

## Record screening and source verification

DOI and PMID supported provisional report reconciliation. Unresolved identifier conflicts and possible versions were flagged for review. The automated screen used titles and available abstracts to match compound names and assay terms and to flag possible roles in quantitative activity, analytical detection, food occurrence or modifying conditions. Roles could overlap. Low-priority records, correction notices, retraction flags and incomplete records were routed for review. The screen applied no automatic exclusions. The priority queue and the targeted original-source reviews were maintained separately. A report could link to several compounds, and several papers could describe the same experiment.

For quantitative extraction we required a source-located original measurement attributable to a named compound and assay endpoint. We recorded material form, concentration and mass or molar basis, vehicle, probe, pH, temperature, integration procedure, Trolox calibration, error definition and independent replicate information where available. We kept extract and mixture measurements separate, checked whether results were secondary or reused, and held conflicting endpoints and denominators. Missing replicate or error definitions were left unresolved. The release records distinguish scoped source verification and conditional model use from final scientific eligibility.

## Assay harmonization and conditional modelling

We used fluorescein-based oxygen radical absorbance capacity (ORAC-FL) expressed in mol Trolox equivalents per mol compound. ORAC-FL measures protection of a fluorescent probe under defined assay conditions [1,2]. We converted compatible mass-normalized values by multiplying µmol Trolox equivalents per gram by the molecular weight in grams per mole and by 10⁻⁶. We applied the same scale factor to reported dispersion. Already molar values were retained. We did not convert values from other assays or reference-normalized indices without a compatible denominator. The target comparison was pH 7.4 and 37 °C. The conditional evidence network also included protocol blocks with other or incompletely documented conditions, allowing comparisons through shared compounds subject to model assumptions.

The response was the natural logarithm of the positive harmonized mean. The design included one coefficient per compound and centered effects for reported study–protocol blocks. Block effects were shrunk using a fixed ridge penalty of 10. Observations were initially weighted by the reciprocal of their report's row count, then multiplied by max(0.10, [1 + (CV/0.25)²]⁻¹), where CV was reported SD divided by the mean. Untyped or missing SD received a multiplier of 1. We normalized the resulting weights to have mean one. This weighting addressed study size and documented within-report dispersion. A comparable sampling variance was not assigned to observations lacking an independently verified replicate count. The conditional and sensitivity cohorts and their source inputs are described in the [model audit](../03_candidate_selection/11_orac_fulltext_followup/README.md).

## Validation and ranking stability

We assessed structure-based extrapolation separately using 36 flavonoids from a single original report, assigned to five scaffold groups. Nested scaffold holdout compared molecular-descriptor and Morgan-fingerprint Ridge models with an intercept-only baseline on the source-reported log ORAC scale. The structure-based estimates did not enter the conditional fit or the candidate retention rule.

We withheld all observations linked to one DOI at a time and refitted the model. We scored predictions for held-out observations whose compound coefficient was estimable from the training design and used no fitted offset for the unseen held-out protocol. We calculated root mean squared error (RMSE) on the log-capacity scale. For model-to-model comparison we used the common estimable rows from the prior cohort.

For the descriptive rankings in the left panel of Fig. 1B, within each DOI–protocol block with at least two compounds we assigned the fraction of peer values below the compound plus half the fraction tied with it. We took the median across blocks within each DOI, then the median across DOIs. We displayed this value alongside the fraction of conditional-model reports with typed SD and the number of conditional-model DOIs. The composition of the comparator set can vary by report.

We ranked compound coefficients in descending order. The expanded conditional rank was the reference. The sensitivity range combined ranks across the available source-inclusion and dispersion-weight scenarios with conservative rank bounds obtained by iteratively deleting each DOI in the two-source cohort. Rank bounds used pairwise contrasts estimable from the reduced design, with unresolved orderings allowed to widen the bounds. We retained a compound for further evaluation when the entire range lay within ten positions of its reference rank and the compound remained present after each deletion. We imposed no fixed cutoff on the reference rank. Material and assay qualification are separate experimental decisions.
