# Methods: discovery, evidence organization and assay-panel preparation

## 1. Scope and candidate discovery

We defined the target domain as individually specified chemicals occurring in foods, beverages or customary culinary ingredients. Undefined mixtures, extracts and aggregate antioxidant measurements were outside the molecular panel. Elemental-nutrient labels without a specified reagent form were retained as context. Endogenous occurrence alone did not preclude dietary occurrence.

We reconstructed the historical class-based enumeration directly from its literal curation declarations and retained its exclusion log. This procedure rebuilt the origin of the 191-name collection without using the finished candidate table or antioxidant ranks as discovery inputs. We separately documented a food-source reopening for glutathione and an ergothioneine coverage gap from primary food-analysis abstracts. These records define a dated, source-bounded collection; database-wide completeness and universal food-chemical coverage were not claimed.

## 2. Identity and evidence mapping

Each retained name was linked to a reference CID, InChIKey, molecular formula and molecular weight using the preserved identity cache or a recorded PubChem property refresh. Query URLs, raw responses, access dates and hashes were retained. Reference identity was distinguished from lot-specific analytical confirmation and exact stereochemical/salt/hydrate form.

The existing 157-candidate five-dimensional framework was joined by stable IDs. D1 activity, D2 reliability, D3 maturity, D4 biological relevance and D5 feasibility retained their original meanings. Profiles outside that snapshot received a coverage label, not a negative or zero value. The latest preserved source-closure and uncertainty audits provided candidate-specific checked-study support. Publication proxies, observation rows and independent studies were not interchanged. Missing uncertainty, source access failures, ambiguous identity and future-year metadata remained explicit.

Unapproved AFF-0061 mechanism observations and historical proposals were not applied or credited as validated targets. Original sources, approved rules and the Frozen Top20 v1.0 remained byte-preserved in the source baseline.

## 3. Transparent review-panel preparation

The working queue required reference identity/formula, retained molecular scope, no specific inherited food-occurrence warning, and at least one checked original pure-compound chemical study. Candidates with at least two confirmed studies were displayed first as a reliability consideration, followed by typed-SD availability. Otherwise candidate ID ordered an administrative 20-slot preview. The complete cutoff evidence stratum was reported to expose ties requiring researcher adjudication.

The ordering was a documented preparation rule rather than a new potency rank, optimized composite score or preregistered clinical decision. No journal-impact weighting, publication-count potency weighting or unapproved integrated score was used. A two-confirmed-study sensitivity analysis was retained without forcing a 20-material result. An independently gated laboratory-ready panel required candidate-specific food-source verification, exact reagent/procurement, solubility/stability, interference clearance and an actual researcher inclusion decision with source, reviewer and date.

## 4. Non-fluorescent assay development

### Capacity-evidence comparison

Quantitative observations were linked by candidate identifier, DOI, original table location, endpoint scale and reported concentration. New original-source checks annotated historical records rather than creating additional independent studies. Log-scale summaries, dose-specific Trolox equivalents and calibration-slope ratios were retained separately. Source-local descriptive ordering was restricted to checked subsets sharing a paper, reporting scale and dose; it was not treated as a global capacity ranking or a significance test. Reference molecular identity, compound-specific food occurrence and the exact experimental material form were assessed as separate fields. Missing source access, error definitions and independent experimental-unit counts remained unresolved rather than receiving zero scores or invented inverse-variance weights.

We compared primary reports of ORAC-PGR, crocin bleaching and ABTS for an aqueous absorbance workflow. For a capacity-primary objective, crocin dose-time protection was identified as the first method-development candidate, ABTS as a distinct complementary response and ORAC-PGR as a reactivity-sensitive comparator. No method was approved. Historical ORAC-FL values were not converted into predicted absorbance-assay outcomes. Published settings were distinguished from unapproved platform parameters. Method searches were date-bounded and source-access scope was recorded.

The planned pilot evaluates exact material form, stock and final solubility, vehicle, stability, spectral overlap, turbidity, precipitation, probe interactions, pH, timing and temperature. Technical replicate wells are nested within independent preparations/days. The analysis code integrates normalized, matched-background absorbance traces by the trapezoidal rule and rejects unresolved counter-screen or time-grid mismatches. A Trolox-equivalent model, formal QC thresholds and experimental conditions require separate validation before an experimental capacity result is reported.

## 5. Reproducibility and manuscript outputs

Numbered research steps link source inputs, decision files, Python/R code, tables and manuscript text. Core evidence tables use Python's standard library; the optional structural analysis additionally requires pinned RDKit and NumPy dependencies. R with ggplot2/svglite exports editable PNG/SVG pairs. The default run uses committed snapshots. Network refresh, manual decisions, supplemental historical figures and convenience Excel export are explicit operations. The QC checks source hashes, input/output identifiers, gate logic, cutoff ties, missing-state treatment, formulas and editable vector structure. Organizational changes do not constitute scientific approval or production database migration.

Primary method references and checked scope: `04_absorbance_assay/inputs/primary_method_registry.json`. Food-source additions: DOI 10.1080/01635589209514173 and 10.1021/jf071328f (abstract-level source checks). Full food-occurrence closure and actual laboratory validation remain future work.

## 6. Exploratory ORAC-FL structure modelling and grouped validation

The researcher-selected first modelling endpoint was molar ORAC-FL capacity; transfer to an absorbance endpoint requires separate paired-assay validation. We extracted the 36 experimental ln(ORAC) entries from Table 3 of Zuveľa et al. [1], excluding the PLS and ANN prediction columns. Reported experimental plus/minus values were retained without assuming SD, SE or independent n. Exponentiated log locations were not treated as arithmetic means. Exact-name mapping identified 16 matches to the current food-candidate universe; the full 36-row source series was used only for exploratory method assessment, without adding unmatched compounds to the experimental shortlist.

Candidate molecular graphs were retrieved from PubChem through stored CIDs or source-name queries, with query provenance and available raw-response hashes [2]. One short-response record was retained as a structured extraction rather than claimed as a raw HTTP cache. Reference-key agreement was distinguished from experimental-material identity. Unspecified stereochemistry was not assigned, and source structures/material forms remained unreviewed where drawings or supplementary records were unavailable. Food occurrence, edible tissue, hydrolysis-derived versus native compounds, source-reported purity and future procurement-lot identity were separate gates; evidence-specific DOI/location fields document each determination.

RDKit 2026.09.1 parsed the 36 candidate graphs. We generated non-isomeric RDKit Murcko scaffolds and generic-framework diagnostics [3,4]; implementation-specific Murcko groups were not assumed identical to every historical framework definition. Molecules sharing constitutional connectivity were constrained to the same group, including Catechin and Epicatechin. Five groups of 23, 8, 3, 1 and 1 compounds defined leave-one-scaffold-group-out assessment. The split addresses structural leakage, following the motivation for scaffold-based evaluation [5], but does not constitute independent-paper validation.

For a simple structural baseline, molecular weight, calculated logP, topological polar surface area and hydrogen-bond donor count were computed using RDKit. Ridge regression predicted experimental ln(ORAC), with an unpenalized intercept. Within each outer training set, feature means and population standard deviations were estimated locally; zero-variance features used a scale of one. The penalty was selected from 0.1, 1, 10 and 100 by inner leave-scaffold-group-out pooled mean squared error, with ties resolved toward the smaller value. Each inner fold refitted scaling and regression without its held-out group. Outer predictions were compared with the mean of the corresponding training labels. We reported per-group and pooled MAE/RMSE on the log scale; small test groups and the dominant scaffold were retained rather than silently omitted. No prediction intervals, external-study performance, final ranking or target-assay conversion were inferred. The implementation is `validate_scaffold_baseline.py` and dependencies are pinned in `requirements_structure.txt`.

### Error diagnosis and post-hoc feature sensitivity

After inspecting the initial outer-fold results, we compared each held-out compound's four descriptor values with the corresponding training-set minimum and maximum. This univariate range check was descriptive and was not treated as a calibrated applicability domain. Descriptor vectors rounded to eight decimal places were grouped to identify collisions: molecules indistinguishable to the baseline feature representation despite different source labels. Such differences were not attributed causally to stereochemistry or interpreted as proof of erroneous measurements.

We then performed a post-hoc sensitivity analysis adding phenolic-OH and aryl-methoxy substructure counts, computed in RDKit [4] using SMARTS `[OX2H][c]` and `[CH3][OX2][c]`, respectively. The same outer scaffold groups and nested training-only penalty selection were retained. Because the added features were selected after inspecting the initial validation results, this comparison is model development rather than a new independent confirmation. No source label was changed, no out-of-range row was removed, and no unverified uncertainty weights were introduced. Scripts `diagnose_scaffold_errors.py` and `validate_scaffold_baseline.py --augmented` regenerate these outputs. Scaffold definitions and validation rationale follow [3–5]; the added chemical patterns are explicit project analysis choices, not a published universal antioxidant score.

### Post-hoc positional-structure sensitivity

We next replaced the descriptor vector with RDKit Morgan fingerprints (radius 2, 1024 binary bits, bond types and ring membership retained, chirality excluded), motivated by circular molecular representations [6]. This was a further post-hoc analysis using the same source labels and outer scaffold groups, not a new validation dataset. Chirality was excluded because source-material stereochemistry remained unresolved; identical representations of stereoisomers were reported explicitly. Ridge regularization, inner group-based penalty selection and training-only feature standardization followed the preceding procedure. For high-dimensional fingerprints the ridge solution was evaluated in equivalent dual form to avoid inversion of the 1024-dimensional feature matrix. Fingerprint settings were not tuned on outer-fold performance. The script option is `validate_scaffold_baseline.py --fingerprint`; per-group errors accompany pooled metrics. This analysis did not establish a broadly applicable QSAR or authorize final candidate ranks.

### Catechin label reconciliation and retrospective external-source queue

Catechin, Epicatechin and Epigallocatechin were checked against the experimental ln(ORAC) column in the original 2019 Table 3 [1]. We compared the exponentiated original log locations with historical extraction values, using an absolute tolerance of 0.001 for their reported rounding. Back-transformed numbers carrying a logarithmic unit label were flagged without changing original records. The models already used the original log column; no training labels were replaced. Exponentiation was not interpreted as recovery of an arithmetic mean or original-scale SD.

For external-source preparation, we selected historical quantitative observations for the 16 exact-name-mapped candidate IDs and excluded the model-development DOI. Paper identities, source locations, scale, experimental protocol and data reuse require verification before a row can become a test label. These previously accessible project records form a retrospective source queue, not a blinded external test set. Test-label fields remain blank until those checks close. No model selection or tuning used the queue's audit values in this step. Source independence is not inferred solely from a different DOI. Metadata-only repository access, including request-copy records, was not counted as full-text recovery. The reproducible implementation is `audit_catechin_and_external_sources.py`.

For Rothe et al. [8], we extracted only the experimental ORAC column of the original Table 6, excluding its literature column and beer-composition measurements. Source-reported catechin hydrate was distinguished from unspecified parent material. Pure-compound replicate counts and error interpretation were not borrowed from beer brewing/spiking experiments. The table's TE label was retained pending normalization review, rather than silently converted to a molar test label.

For Ishimoto et al. [7], the original advance-view article provided two explicit numerical means in the Results text and named the catechin stereoisomers. We recorded the source version, locator and assay protocol. Although the article defined its plotted error bars as SD, no numeric SD was inferred from the plotted bars. These source checks were separated from approval as common-target test labels; protocol differences and prior exposure remained documented. No experimental volumes or concentrations were transcribed from PDF text where micro-unit parsing could be ambiguous.

The targeted Rothe source audit [8] checked the reagent list, assay equations, statistical scope, individual-compound discussion and Table 6 caption/headers. Calibration in TE was distinguished from an explicit per-mole-compound denominator. The pure-compound normalization formula, hydrate concentration basis, individual epicatechin reagent identity and pure-compound error definition were not established in those sections. Four measurements were not counted as four independent preparations. Original means and reported plus/minus values were retained without conversion, inverse-variance weighting or test-label release. Publisher text and parsed PDF were inspected; failed PDF screenshot requests were not counted as visual verification.

### Retrospective protocol-shift pressure test

The researcher authorized a separately reported diagnostic using the two explicit molar means from Ishimoto et al. [7]. Morgan-fingerprint ridge regression retained the fixed representation described above. Penalty selection used only the 36 development labels and leave-scaffold-group-out pooled MSE across 0.1, 1, 10 and 100, with fold-specific scaling. The model was fitted on all development labels before reading external means. Predictions and a development-mean baseline were compared with natural logarithms of reported arithmetic means. These differ from the modelled log-location summary. Both compounds occurred in development and received identical non-chiral representations. Individual signed residuals and descriptive MAE/RMSE were reported for two observations without variance weighting or confidence intervals. No model tuning, ranking or formal external/new-structure validation used these outcomes. Numeric SD was not digitized. Analysis authorization does not approve evidence records. Script: `run_retrospective_pressure_test.py`; offline reproduction: `run_model_checks.py`.

### Matched-calibrator source audit

For Carvalho et al. [9], cached original BioC tables and Methods traced quercetin TE to the matched ethanol-medium AUC slopes (99.22/5.97), rather than the buffer Trolox slope (5.25). We retained the source ratio and its reported uncertainty without deriving SD or effective n. Hydrate concentration basis and development-material equivalence remained separate gates. This recheck added no independent study or model label. The source-check JSON records offsets and cache hash; `audit_catechin_and_external_sources.py` and `assess_external_comparability.py` regenerate the queue and decisions.

### External evidence readiness and design connectivity

We audited the existing 28-observation external queue across ten DOIs without treating historical values or search snippets as recovered original measurements. Original numeric access, endpoint normalization, material identity, error definition, and data-generating-study independence were recorded separately. The Froldi source [10] established a molar baicalein mean with SEM and a 3–6-experiment figure caption; no row-specific n or SD was imputed. Unresolved assay calculation details remained explicit. A different DOI or absence of duplicated row keys did not establish independent measurements.

`build_external_validation_readiness.py` builds a bipartite compound–paper graph separately for audit-only, numeric-rechecked, molar-estimator-traced and eligible records. Each unique compound–paper pair is one edge. For an unweighted additive compound-plus-paper indicator design, the design rank is the number of vertices minus connected components; edge count minus rank reports its topological redundancy, not residual assay variance. Repeated rows are not independent edges. We also include the 36 development rows as a separate anchor analysis, preserving unmatched compounds as development-only identifiers rather than adding them to the food-candidate universe. Graph connectivity does not establish common protocols, equivalent log estimands, independent studies or valid predictions. No study effects or cross-paper rankings were fitted from this diagnostic. These explicit project diagnostics require no learned weights or parameter tuning.
